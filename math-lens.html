Math Lens

Definition 1. A math lens is a bookmarklet for the HTML versions of arXiv papers. Hover a symbol and it shows the sentence where that symbol is defined. Hover a link to a theorem, equation or citation and it shows what the link points to.

Spectral Gaps for Averaging Operators on Finite Groups

Abstract

We study the operator Tμ acting on functions on a finite group G and show that its spectral gap λ controls the mixing time.

1 Introduction

Random walks on groups are a classical topic [1]. In this paper G will always be a finite group and μ a probability measure on G. Our main result is Theorem 3.2, which bounds ‖Tμn⁢f‖2 in terms of the gap λ defined in (3).

2 Preliminaries and notation

Let G be a finite group with identity e, and let ℱ denote the space of complex-valued functions on G. We write |G| for the order of G. For f∈ℱ and 1≤p<∞ we set

‖f‖p≔(1|G|⁢∑g∈G|f⁢(g)|p)1/p. (1)

A probability measure on G is a function μ:G→[0,1] with ∑gμ⁢(g)=1.

Definition 2.1.

Given a probability measure μ on G, the averaging operator Tμ:ℱ→ℱ is defined by

(Tμ⁢f)⁢(x)=∑g∈Gμ⁢(g)⁢f⁢(x⁢g), (2)

where x∈G and f∈ℱ. We say μ is symmetric if μ⁢(g)=μ⁢(g−1) for all g.

Denote by F the subspace of ℱ consisting of functions with mean zero. The spectral gap of μ is

λ=1−supf∈F,f≠0‖Tμ⁢f‖2‖f‖2, (3)

where the supremum is over nonzero f. Throughout, f^n denotes the n-th Fourier coefficient of f, and f′ is the function x↦f⁢(x−1).

Remark 2.2.

The letter F (mean-zero functions) should not be confused with ℱ.

3 Main results

Lemma 3.1.

Let μ be symmetric. Then ‖Tμ⁢f‖2≤‖f‖2 for every f∈ℱ.

Proof.

Fix f∈ℱ and let x∈G. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

|(Tμ⁢f)⁢(x)|2 ≤∑gμ⁢(g)⁢|f⁢(x⁢g)|2 (4)
=∑y∈Gμ⁢(x−1⁢y)⁢|f⁢(y)|2.

Summing (4) over x and using ∑xμ⁢(x−1⁢y)=1 gives the claim. ∎

Theorem 3.2.

Let μ be a symmetric probability measure on G with spectral gap λ>0. Then for every f∈F and every n≥1,

‖Tμn⁢f‖2≤(1−λ)n⁢‖f‖2. (5)
Proof.

Averaging preserves the mean of a function, so Tμ maps F to itself, and λ≥0 by Lemma 3.1. Let n≥1 and set h=Tμn−1⁢f. By the definition (3) of λ we have ‖Tμ⁢h‖2≤(1−λ)⁢‖h‖2, and induction on n gives (5). ∎

Corollary 3.3.

For every ε>0 there is N with ‖Tμn⁢f‖2≤ε⁢‖f‖2 for all n≥N and f∈F.

Proof.

Let ε>0 and choose N≥log⁡(1/ε)/λ. Then Theorem 3.2 and the inequality (1−λ)n≤e−λ⁢n give the result. Note that here e is Euler’s number, not the identity of G. ∎

4 An example

Let G=ℤ/m⁢ℤ and let μ be uniform on {±1}. Here xk=2⁢π⁢k/m for 0≤k<m, and the eigenvalues of Tμ are cos⁡xk, with the characters as eigenfunctions. For odd m the largest value of |cos⁡xk| with k≠0 is cos⁡(π/m), hence λ=1−cos⁡(π/m)≈π2/(2⁢m2), and Corollary 3.3 applies with N of order m2. In Fourier terms, Tμk multiplies f^n from Section 2 by (cos⁡xn)k.

§2 Install

Bookmarklet

  1. Show your bookmarks bar: Ctrl Shift B, or ⌘ Shift B on a Mac.
  2. Drag the Math Lens button at the top of this page onto the bar.
  3. If dragging doesn’t work, copy the code, add a new bookmark, and paste it into the URL field.

Always on (userscript)

  1. Install a userscript manager such as Tampermonkey or Violentmonkey.
  2. Create a new script and replace its contents with the copied code.
  3. The lens then starts by itself on every arxiv.org/html and ar5iv paper.

§3 Controls

Hover a symbol
After a short pause a card shows where it is defined and where it first appears. Every use of it on the page is highlighted.
Click a symbol
Pins the card so you can move into it. Tapping does the same on a phone.
Tabs
For a symbol like f̂ₙ, switch between f̂ₙ, f̂ and f.
Other candidates
When several sentences look like definitions, pick another one to read it in place.
Context
The bar at the bottom of each card sets how much surrounding text you see: Sentence, Paragraph or Wider (the whole theorem or proof, or the paragraphs on either side). A theorem link offers Statement, + Lead-in and + Proof. Whatever you pick becomes the default for later cards. The + and − keys do the same.
‹ ›
Jump to the previous or next use of the symbol.
Go to ↗
Scrolls to that spot. A ↩ Back button in the corner returns you to where you were reading.
Esc
Closes the card.

§4 How it picks a definition

  • It reads the sentence around every use of a symbol and scores phrasings that introduce one: Let G be, denote by F, where λ is, for every ε > 0, there is N, and formulas with :=. Only the part of a formula left of its first relation counts as being defined, so in let x ∈ G it is x, not G.
  • It prefers the nearest definition before the place you are reading: the same paragraph first, then the same theorem and its proof, then the main text and Definition environments. That is why the two e’s in the demo resolve differently.
  • Symbols are matched with their font and decorations, so F, 𝓕 and F̂ are three different symbols. Exponents are set aside, so hovering x in x² looks up x.
  • When no sentence looks like a definition, the card shows the first appearance instead.
  • Everything runs in your browser. The paper is never sent anywhere.

§5 Limits

  • It needs the HTML version of a paper, which arXiv and ar5iv generate with LaTeXML. It does not work on PDFs. On an arXiv abstract page, clicking the bookmark offers to open the HTML version.
  • Some papers fail to convert to HTML, and some conversions mangle a few formulas. The lens can only see what the HTML contains.
  • It finds definitions from the wording around a formula, so it misses ones written only in words or hidden inside custom macros. It can also pick a sentence that merely mentions a symbol. The “Other candidates” row and the first appearance are there for those cases.
